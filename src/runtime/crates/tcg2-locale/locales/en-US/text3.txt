[GlossaryDeckDescriptionText]
The set of cards you duel with is
called a "deck."
A deck must contain exactly 60
cards.
A deck may not have more than 4
cards with the same name.
Building your own deck and dueling
with it--that's the real joy of
Pokémon cards!

[GlossaryDeckPileDescriptionText] full-width
The pile of cards
you will draw
from is called
the "Deck." At
the start of your
turn, you draw
1 card from your
Deck. If your Deck
has no cards left
and you can't draw
a card, you lose
the duel.

[GlossaryDiscardPileDescriptionText]
The place where used cards go is
called the "Discard Pile."
Putting a card there is called
"discarding" it.
Using the Check command, you can
look at the cards in your Discard
Pile, and in your opponent's as
well.

[GlossaryHandDescriptionText] full-width
The cards each
player holds are
called their
"Hand." There is
no limit to the
number of cards in
your Hand. You can
hold 10 or even 20
cards.

[GlossaryArenaDescriptionText] full-width
The spot for the
Pokémon that is
fighting right now
is the "Arena."
The Pokémon in the
Arena is called
the "Active
Pokémon." The game
is played by using
the Active Pokémon
in the Arena.

[GlossaryBenchDescriptionText] full-width
The place where
Pokémon wait to
switch in for the
Pokémon in the
Arena is called
the "Bench."
Normally, you
can have up to 5
Pokémon waiting on
your Bench.

[GlossaryActivePokemonDescriptionText] full-width
The Pokémon
in the Arena
is called the
"Active Pokémon."
You use your
Active Pokémon's
attacks to defeat
your opponent's
Pokémon.

[GlossaryBenchedPokemonDescriptionText]
The Pokémon on the Bench, waiting to
switch in for the Pokémon in the
Arena, are called "Benched Pokémon."
When the Pokémon in the Arena is
gone, you choose one of your Benched
Pokémon to put into the Arena.
If you have no Benched Pokémon to
put into the Arena then, you lose.

[GlossaryPrizesDescriptionText]
The cards used to count how many of
your opponent's Pokémon you have
defeated are called "Prizes."
Each time you defeat one of your
opponent's Pokémon, you can take 1
Prize.
Take all of your Prizes, and you win
the duel.
So the goal of the game is to defeat
as many of your opponent's Pokémon
as the number of Prizes you set.

[GlossaryDamageCountersDescriptionText]
"Damage counters" show how much
damage a Pokémon has taken.
1 damage counter stands for 10
points of damage.
If 3 damage counters are placed on
a Pokémon with 30 HP, it has taken
30 damage, so its HP is 0, and it
is Knocked Out.

[GlossaryEnergyCardsDescriptionText]
"Energy cards" are cards that
represent Energy.
There are 7 types of Energy in all:
<GRASS>Grass, <FIRE>Fire, <WATER>Water,
<LIGHTNING>Lightning, <PSYCHIC>Psychic,
<FIGHTING>Fighting, and <COLORLESS>Colorless.
You can play only 1 Energy card from
your Hand during your turn.

[GlossaryTrainerCardsDescriptionText]
"Trainer cards" are cards that help
you out.
Each Trainer card has a different
effect, so be sure to read each
card's description.
During your turn, you can play as
many Trainer cards from your Hand
as you like.

[GlossaryBasicPokemonText]
Among Pokémon cards, the Pokémon
that others evolve from are called
"Basic Pokémon."
For example, Charmander, Squirtle,
and Bulbasaur are Basic Pokémon.
Only Basic Pokémon can be put
directly from your Hand into the
Arena or onto your Bench.

[GlossaryEvolutionCardsDescriptionText]
Cards showing Pokémon that have
evolved from Basic Pokémon are
called "Evolution cards."
There are Stage 1 and Stage 2
Evolution cards. Squirtle is a Basic
Pokémon, Wartortle is Stage 1, and
Blastoise is Stage 2.
You can't play a Stage 1 card unless
its Basic Pokémon is in play, and
you can't play a Stage 2 card unless
its Stage 1 Pokémon is in play.

[GlossaryDarkEvolutionCardsDescriptionText]
Among Evolution cards, there are
"Dark Evolution cards." These are
cards with "Dark" in the name, like
Dark Charmeleon and Dark Charizard.
Like normal ones, they evolve from
Basic to Stage 1 to Stage 2.
If a Pokémon evolved into a Dark
Stage 1, its Stage 2 must be Dark
too. If it evolved into a normal
Stage 1, its Stage 2 can't be Dark.

[GlossaryAttackDescriptionText]
By using "attacks," a Pokémon can
hit the opponent's Pokémon.
To use an attack, a Pokémon needs
Energy. The Energy needed differs
from attack to attack.
Also, only the Active Pokémon in
the Arena can use attacks.

[GlossaryPokemonPowerDescriptionText]
Unlike attacks, a "Pokémon Power"
can be used even by Pokémon on the
Bench.
There are many kinds: some work just
by having the Pokémon in play, some
work when you choose them with the
Power command, and some can be
used only once, when the Pokémon is
played from your Hand.
Be sure to read each card's text!

[GlossaryWeaknessDescriptionText] full-width
Some Pokémon have
a "Weakness."
A Pokémon with
a Weakness takes
double damage from
Pokémon of its
Weakness color
(type).

[GlossaryResistanceDescriptionText] full-width
Some Pokémon have
"Resistance."
A Pokémon with
Resistance takes
30 less damage
from Pokémon of
its Resistance
color (type).

[GlossaryRetreatDescriptionText]
Choosing "Flee" makes your Active
Pokémon "retreat" to the Bench, so
you can switch the Pokémon in the
Arena with one on your Bench.
Retreating also takes Energy. The
amount needed differs from Pokémon
to Pokémon, so discard as many
Energy cards as it needs.

[GlossaryPoisonedDescriptionText]
A Poisoned Pokémon keeps taking 10
damage between turns.
To cure Poison, you can retreat the
Pokémon to your Bench, or use the
Trainer card "Full Heal."
You can also cure it by evolving or
devolving the Pokémon.

[GlossaryParalyzedDescriptionText]
A Paralyzed Pokémon can't do
anything. It can't retreat, and it
can't use Pokémon Powers or attacks.
Paralysis wears off by itself at the
end of its owner's turn.
Using "Full Heal," evolving, or
devolving also cures Paralysis.
It is also cured if the Paralyzed
Pokémon is moved to the Bench.

[GlossaryAsleepDescriptionText]
An Asleep Pokémon can't do anything.
It can't retreat, attack, or use
Pokémon Powers.
Between turns, flip a coin: if
heads, it wakes up; if tails, it
stays Asleep.
Using "Full Heal," evolving, or
devolving also cures Sleep.

[GlossaryConfusedDescriptionText]
When a Confused Pokémon attacks, you
flip a coin.
If heads, the attack works normally.
If tails, the attack fails! In its
confusion, the Pokémon attacks
itself and takes 20 damage.
You also flip a coin when it tries
to retreat: if heads, it retreats;
if tails, it can't.
Confusion is cured in the same ways
as Poison.

[GlossaryDecision1TakenAllPrizesDescriptionText]
When a Pokémon's HP runs out, it is
"Knocked Out."
When you knock out an opponent's
Pokémon, you take 1 Prize for each
Pokémon Knocked Out.
The first player to take all their
Prizes this way wins.
So if you knock out as many of your
opponent's Pokémon as the Prizes set
at the start of the duel, you win!

[GlossaryDecision2DeckedOutDescriptionText]
If your Deck has no cards left when
you draw at the start of your turn,
you can't draw, and you lose the
duel.
Many card effects make you draw
more cards from your Deck, so keep
an eye on how many cards are left in
your Deck.

[GlossaryDecision3RunOutOfPokemonDescriptionText] full-width
When your Active
Pokémon is Knocked
Out, you lose
if there are no
Pokémon on your
Bench to put into
the Arena next.
So always try to
keep some Pokémon
waiting on your
Bench.

[GlossarySuddenDeathDescriptionText]
If you and your opponent take all
your Prizes at the same time, or
neither of you can put a Pokémon
into the Arena, the duel is a "tie"
and goes to "Sudden Death."
In Sudden Death, you duel again
with 1 Prize each.
If there's still no winner, Sudden
Death is repeated.

[GlossaryChlorophyllDescriptionText]
Special Rule 1
"Chlorophyll"

In this duel, Grass Pokémon can't be
Poisoned, Paralyzed, Confused, or
Asleep.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryThunderChargeDescriptionText]
Special Rule 2
"Thunder Charge"

In this duel, Lightning Pokémon's
attacks do 10 more damage to the
Defending Pokémon.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryFlameArmorDescriptionText]
Special Rule 3
"Flame Armor"

In this duel, Fire Pokémon aren't
affected by Weakness when they take
damage from Water Pokémon's attacks.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossarySmallBenchDescriptionText]
Special Rule 4
"Small Bench"

In this duel, each Bench has only 3
spaces.
(Each player can have up to 3
` Benched Pokémon.)`

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryRunningWaterDescriptionText]
Special Rule 5
"Running Water"

In this duel, Water Pokémon need 1
less Energy to retreat.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryEarthPowerDescriptionText]
Special Rule 6
"Earth Power"

In this duel, damage done by
Fighting Pokémon isn't reduced by
Resistance.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryLowResistanceDescriptionText]
Special Rule 7
"Low Resistance"

In this duel, Resistance reduces
damage by only 10 instead of 30.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryEnergyReturnDescriptionText]
Special Rule 8
"Energy Return"

In this duel, when a Pokémon in play
is discarded, the basic Energy cards
attached to it return to its owner's
Hand.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryToughEscapeDescriptionText]
Special Rule 9
"Tough Escape"

In this duel, an Active Pokémon must
discard 1 more Energy to retreat.

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[GlossaryBlackHoleDescriptionText]
Special Rule 10
"Discard Black Hole"

In this duel, discarded cards can't
be used again until the duel ends.
(The Discard Pile is always empty.)

*Special Rules apply only when your
` opponent calls for them.`

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice1Description1Text]
Basic Pokémon cards are the
foundation of all Pokémon cards!
Only Basic Pokémon can be put down
at the start of a duel, or put
straight into play. No Pokémon, no
duel! And without Basic Pokémon,
you can't play Evolution cards!

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice1Description2Text] full-width
Hmm, let me see...
You'll want at
least 12 Basic
Pokémon cards in
your deck!

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice2Description1Text]
Don't forget to put Energy cards in
your deck!
Without Energy, Pokémon can't do a
thing!!
They can't attack, and they can't
even retreat from the Arena.
You need Energy for everything!

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice2Description2Text]
The color of your Energy matters,
too.
Match your Energy's colors to your
Pokémon's colors.
Let's see... It's best to put at
least as many Energy cards in your
deck as Pokémon cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice3Description1Text]
There are 3 kinds of Pokémon cards:
Basic Pokémon, Stage 1, and Stage 2.
Basic Pokémon can be put straight
into play, but a Stage 1 can't be
played unless its Basic Pokémon is
in play, and a Stage 2 can't be
played unless its Stage 1 is in
play.

[DeckDiagnosisStep1Advice3Description2Text]
In a deck, it's best to have the
most Basic Pokémon, then fewer
Stage 1s, and fewer still Stage 2s.
Something like 4 Basics, 3 Stage 1s,
and 2 Stage 2s.

[DeckDiagnosisStep2Advice1DescriptionText]
Bill and Professor Oak are great
cards when you're aiming for a big
comeback!
Put about 2 Professor Oak cards in
your deck, and try about 2 Bill
cards, too.

[DeckDiagnosisStep2Advice2DescriptionText]
Next in importance are Potion and
Switch.
Potion and Switch are easy to use,
so try adding them.
Try about 3 Potion cards and about
2 Switch cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep2Advice3DescriptionText]
Your opponent's attacks can affect
your Pokémon with Special Conditions
like Poison, Paralysis, Sleep, or
Confusion.
When that happens, use the Trainer
card Full Heal!
It's good to have 1 or 2 in your
deck.

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice1Description1Text]
First, try building a deck of 2
colors plus Colorless.
2 colors plus Colorless is simple
and easy to duel with.
Your Energy colors rarely mismatch,
and it's good for dueling while
covering each other's Weaknesses.
Plan how to make up for Weaknesses!

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice1Description2Text]
For example...
Take a deck built around Lightning
Pokémon. They're weak to Fighting
Pokémon, so add Psychic Pokémon or
Colorless Pokémon (Flying type),
which are strong against Fighting.

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice1Description3Text]
And take a deck built around Fire
Pokémon. They're weak to Water
Pokémon, so add Lightning Pokémon,
which are strong against Water.
Lightning Pokémon are weak to
Fighting, so add a few Colorless
(Flying type) Pokémon to cover them.

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice1Description4Text] full-width
So that makes a
deck of Fire +
Lightning +
Colorless (Flying
type).

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice2Description1Text]
With some ingenuity, a 3-color deck
can be a lot of fun!
In "multicolor decks" (decks with
Pokémon of 2 or more colors), like
3-color decks, it's good to add 2 to
4 Energy Search cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice2Description2Text] full-width
For example...
Fire + Lightning
+ Grass works
surprisingly well.
Looks like a
traffic light,
though... Ha ha!

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice3Description1Text]
Whatever happens, don't worry about
losing. Duel again and again, and
think about what went wrong.
If you lost because no Pokémon came
up, add more Basic Pokémon. If you
lost because no Energy cards came
up, add more Energy cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep3Advice3Description2Text]
Overall balance matters most, so
keep an eye on it.
You can use fewer colors in your
deck, use fewer Evolution cards, or
use Trainer cards to draw lots of
cards. There are many ways! The key
is to try different things and have
fun dueling.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description1Text]
"Traits of Each Color"

Many Fire Pokémon have attacks that
discard Fire Energy, so in decks
with lots of Fire Pokémon, add about
2 Energy Retrieval cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description2Text]
Many Lightning Pokémon have attacks
that also do damage to themselves.
In decks like that, it's good to add
about 2 Defender cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description3Text]
Many Grass Pokémon have Stage 2
evolutions.
So in decks like that, add cards
that help your Pokémon evolve.
About 2 each of Pokémon Trader and
Pokémon Breeder would be good.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description4Text]
Many Water Pokémon have attacks
that do more damage to the opponent
when you add more Water Energy.
In decks like that, it's good to put
in plenty of Water Energy cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description5Text]
Quite a few Fighting Pokémon need
lots of Energy to retreat.
In decks with lots of Fighting
Pokémon, try adding 2 to 4 Switch
cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice1Description6Text]
Psychic Pokémon's damage is
sometimes blocked by Resistance, so
in decks like that, try adding 2 to
4 Gust of Wind cards.

In decks with lots of Colorless
Pokémon, you'll want 4 Double
Colorless Energy cards.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice2Description1Text]
Colorless Pokémon can use attacks
with any color of Energy, so they
are easy to use in any deck.
Also remember that Normal-type and
Flying-type Pokémon have different
Resistances.

[DeckDiagnosisStep4Advice2Description2Text]
In decks with many Colorless
Pokémon, add 2 to 4 Double Colorless
Energy cards.
Even without Colorless Pokémon,
Pokémon with attacks that use

decks easier to use.
